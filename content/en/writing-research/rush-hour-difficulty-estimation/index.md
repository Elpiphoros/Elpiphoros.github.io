## Overview

**Difficulty Estimation of Rush Hour** is a short research paper written for the *Scientific Perspectives on Game and Media Technology* course at Utrecht University. The project explores how the difficulty of levels in the puzzle game **Rush Hour** can be estimated through measurable game features.

The paper proposes two approaches: a **weighted linear difficulty function** and a **weighted nonlinear difficulty function**. The linear function combines selected features directly, while the nonlinear function introduces combined measures to better capture interactions between features that may influence perceived difficulty.

The paper breaks difficulty down into different types of game features, including initial features, solution features, and dynamic features. These include factors such as grid size, number of vehicles, number of trucks and sedans, perpendicular cars, optimal solution length, and counterintuitive moves.

## Research Focus

The main focus of the paper was to examine how puzzle difficulty can be described through formal features of a game level. Rush Hour was used as the case study because its levels have clear initial configurations, movement rules, and solution states.

The proposed nonlinear difficulty function was designed to capture aspects that a purely linear model may miss, such as:

- the balance between trucks and sedans,
- the ratio between occupied and available board space,
- the interaction between exit-direction cars and perpendicular cars,
- the relationship between relevant cars and all cars on the board.

These combined measures were intended to better represent movement restriction, road complexity, and the actual relevance of different cars to the solution path.
