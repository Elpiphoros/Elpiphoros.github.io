## Overview

**Comparing Two Typing Methods** is a short HCI research paper comparing two one-handed input methods on a mobile soft keyboard: **keypress typing** and **swipe typing**. The study explored how these two methods affect typing speed, accuracy, and perceived usability.

The project used a within-subjects experiment in which each participant completed typing tests with both input methods. The typing tests were conducted using Monkeytype on an iPhone, and participants completed a modified System Usability Scale questionnaire after each method.

## Research Focus

The study focused on two main aspects of mobile text input:

- **Typing performance**, measured through words per minute and accuracy.
- **User experience**, measured through usability feedback, perceived speed, perceived accuracy, confidence, and ease of use.

Although the sample size was small, the results suggested a useful difference between objective performance and subjective experience. Participants showed similar accuracy across both methods, but keypress typing was generally faster. The questionnaire responses also suggested that participants felt more confident and efficient when using the keypress method.

The study therefore did not provide strong evidence that swipe typing was a better accessibility feature in this setup, but it highlighted the importance of familiarity, learning time, and subjective confidence when evaluating input methods.
