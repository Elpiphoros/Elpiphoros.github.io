## Overview

**Fundamental Research on a Domino Reconfiguration Puzzle** is a short theoretical research paper written for the *Scientific Perspectives on Game and Media Technology* course at Utrecht University. The paper studies a puzzle in which a board is covered by domino pieces except for two empty squares, and the goal is to transform one domino configuration into another through a set of valid moves.

The project was inspired by research on the **Gourds** sliding-block puzzle. This paper focuses on its formal properties: what kinds of boards are valid, what moves are allowed, and under what conditions one configuration can be transformed into another.

## Research Focus

The paper defines the puzzle using several formal components, including valid boards, dual graphs, Hamiltonian cycles, and valid domino moves. The allowed moves include straight slides, 90-degree turns, sideways slides, and pivots.

The central result of the paper is that, under the stated board conditions, **any two configurations of dominoes on the board can be transformed into each other**. The proof is built through a sequence of lemmas showing that dominoes can be moved onto a Hamiltonian cycle, that a U-configuration exists, and that such a configuration can be used to reverse or reorder dominoes.

The paper also discusses further cases, including boards that are 2-connected and admit a Hamiltonian cycle but still do not allow arbitrary reconfiguration when holes are present, as well as hole-free boards that do not admit a Hamiltonian cycle.
